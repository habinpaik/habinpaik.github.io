A second paragraph is a good place to say how your fields meet — how the engineering, the physics and the reporting feed one another, and what each of them lets you see that the others would miss.

A third paragraph can go into the work itself: a project you are in the middle of, what it is trying to find out, and the step you are on right now. Concrete details carry further than adjectives here — a measurement, a draft, a conversation that changed your mind.

A fourth can look a little further ahead: the kind of problem you hope to be working on in a few years, the people or places you would like to learn from, and what you would want someone reading this page to get in touch with you about.

Keep each paragraph to a single idea, and let the page grow as you do; it reads best when it sounds like you talking, not like a résumé.
